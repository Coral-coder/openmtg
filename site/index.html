A theme for every world

Your deck's main set picks the look of the table: colors, type, frames, the backdrop and the sounds. Each theme is drawn from scratch and inspired by its setting; pick one in Settings to always use it.

Classic

Dark wood, brass and parchment, for every set.

Starship console

Rounded elbows and a starfield, for the final frontier.

Crystal

Deep-blue windows and crystal glints, for a world of summons.

Four elements

Ink-wash paper and brush strokes: water, earth, fire, air.

Web-slinger

Halftone panels and bold outlines, city-night red and blue.

Built on the Comprehensive Rules

The engine is organised by the rules themselves, not around one card at a time.

The stack and priority

Respond to anything at instant speed. The table stops when you can act and passes for you when you can't.

Layers and timestamps

Continuous effects apply in the order the rules say: copy, control, type, color, abilities, power and toughness.

Replacement and prevention

Enters tapped, enters with counters, double damage, prevent damage, "if it would die, exile it instead".

Full combat

First and double strike, trample, deathtouch, menace, flying and reach, multiple blockers, lifelink.

Triggers and state-based actions

Enters, dies, attacks, casts, upkeep and end step, landfall, prowess, ward; the legend rule and lethal damage.

Costs

Kicker, flashback, additional and alternative costs, X spells, cost reductions, sacrifices and Phyrexian mana.

Multiplayer with no server

A game is a random seed plus an ordered list of moves. Every device runs the same deterministic engine, so a phone and a browser always agree; each move carries a fingerprint of the game after it.

  1. Online, by code. One player creates a six-letter code, the other types it. Moves travel encrypted through public Nostr relays; a match can continue days later.
  2. Nearby, on phones. Same Wi‑Fi (or type the host's IP address), or Bluetooth with no network at all.
  3. Crash-proof. Every move is saved as it's made. When devices reconnect they compare move logs and go back to the last move they share.
  4. Fair. Online and rated games only allow cards the engine fully supports, and both devices check every move.
